Overpass Callouts & Map Guide 
By Josh Lingenfelter 
A Berlin park and canal complex with two very different bombsites. Overpass rewards map control, creative utility, and the best-timed rotations in Counter-Strike. 
Map Control Creative Utility Two-Faced Layout Reserve 
Overpass Overview & History 
Overpass is a Valve-made map added to CS:GO in December 2013, set around a Berlin park, a canal, and the concrete underbelly of a motorway overpass. It spent a decade as an Active Duty regular before a rockier CS2 spell: rotated out for Dust 2 in April 2024, restored in July 2025 in place of Anubis, and rotated out again on July 8, 2026, when Cache arrived with the Season 5 update. It currently sits in the reserve pool but remains fully playable in Competitive matchmaking. 
The map is really two maps stitched together. The A half is an open park: long grass sightlines from Playground and Long toward a raised site with Bank, Truck, and Toilets cover. The B half is a vertical concrete pit: Monster and Short tunnels feeding a canal site watched by the towering Heaven platform. Few maps demand such different skills at each end. 
Overpass is famous for asymmetric map control: Ts start close to A but far from B, while CT rotations between sites are fast through Connector. That geometry produces the map's signature move — the A-presence fake into a fast B hit through Monster — and makes information the most valuable currency on the map.

How to Play Overpass T-Side 
T-side Overpass starts with the fountain/mid area. A standard default posts two players toward Long and Party/Toilets, one watching Connector, and two controlling Monster's entrance. From there the side is a patience game: CT aggression on Overpass is common, and punishing an over-extended Toilets or Monster push is worth more than any set execute. 
A hits split Long and Short/Toilets: smoke Bank and Truck, molotov the default boxes and Heaven-side stairs, and cross the open ground in one wave. Bathrooms (Toilets) is the tempo route — quiet walks through it put Ts on top of the site without crossing the park's long sightlines. 
B is about Monster: once Ts own the tunnel, the site can be hit from Monster and Short/Water simultaneously, with a Heaven smoke mandatory on every serious execute. Post-plant, the classic setup holds from Monster, Pit, and Graffiti with the bomb planted for Barrels. Standard lineups for both sites are on our lineups page. 
How to Play Overpass CT-Side 
The standard CT split is 2-1-2 with heavy early information play: the B pair often starts with a Monster or Short check, the Connector player supports both halves, and the A pair holds Long and Toilets at range. Overpass CTs are the most aggressive in the game because the map rewards knowing where the five Ts are — every safe round the Ts get, the scarier their late hit becomes. 
On A, play the site high-low: one player around Bank/Truck at range, one closer to Toilets or the site boxes. Long pushes die to a well-placed molotov and a crossfire; the real danger is the quiet Toilets squeeze, so keep utility for it and re-check the bathrooms often. 
On B, Heaven is the kingmaker: the platform sees the whole site and both entrances. Anchor with one player on-site (Pillar/Toxic/Graffiti) and one Heaven, and remember water slows and telegraphs pushes through the canal. Retakes come from Heaven and Connector/Water together — flash down, clear Pit and Barrels in sequence, and watch Monster for the post-plant hold. 
Key Duels & Angles 
Long park cross vs Bank AWP 
The A half's defining fight: a CT AWP behind Bank or Truck punishes any dry crossing of the park. Ts answer with the Rock/Tree smoke wall or by routing through Toilets instead. 
Toilets squeeze vs close holds 
The bathroom corridor produces point-blank fights with CTs holding the exits from Flowers or the site. Sound discipline decides who gets the first shot — running through Toilets is a death sentence. 
Monster exit vs Heaven 
Ts exiting Monster are exposed to Heaven's elevated angle across the site. The Heaven smoke is mandatory; CTs re-take the angle the moment it fades, so plant fast or re-smoke. 
Connector control 
The Connector corridor is both the CT rotation route and the T flank path. Squeaky door audio, boost spots, and molotov timings make this the map's quiet mid-round battleground. 
Water fights (B canal) 
Pushes through the canal are slowed and telegraphed by water noise, but the low angle under B's platform surprises static anchors. Both sides use it late in rounds when utility is spent. 
Common Strategies & Executes 
The executes below lean on standard utility — practice the exact throws on our Overpass smoke & flash lineups page. 
T-Side Attack 
A Split (Long + Toilets) 
Smoke Bank and Truck, molotov Flowers, and hit the site from Long and Toilets simultaneously — the two-direction entry breaks the high-low defence. 
Monster + Short B Execute 
Smoke Heaven and Sandbags, then enter from Monster and B Short at once. Plant for Barrels and hold from Monster, Pit, and Graffiti. 
A Fake → Fast B 
Throw A utility at Bank/Truck with two players showing at Long, then sprint the pack through Monster before Heaven resets. Overpass's signature round-stealer. 
Connector Take 
Control Lower Tunnels and Connector mid-round to threaten both halves at once; the CTs must guess which side the squeeze hits. 
CT-Side Defense 
Early Info Push 
The B pair checks Monster/Short with a flash at round start while Toilets is peeked from Flowers — then everyone resets to standard holds with the T positions mapped. 
Heaven Anchor System 
Heaven player calls the B half alone from the platform, with the site anchor playing off Pillar and the Connector flex arriving through Water for retakes.

Overpass in Pro Play 
Overpass built a reputation as a tactician's map across a decade of pro play: deep CT aggression, elaborate A fakes into Monster hits, and some of the most famous clutch moments in Major history happened on its canal site. Teams with strong utility discipline and mid-round calling historically over-performed here. 
Its July 2026 rotation out of Active Duty ended a long competitive chapter, but reserve-pool maps have returned before — Overpass itself came back once already in 2025. The tracked pro stats above reflect its record while in the pool.

Frequently Asked Questions Is Overpass still playable in CS2? Yes. Overpass left the Active Duty pool on July 8, 2026, when Cache replaced it in the Season 5 update, but it remains in the game and playable in Competitive matchmaking as a reserve-pool map. Premier mode, however, only uses the seven Active Duty maps. Why do teams fake A and hit B on Overpass? The map's geometry makes it the natural play: Ts spawn close to the A park, so showing bodies and utility there is cheap, while Monster offers a direct tunnel to B once the CTs commit. Because Heaven takes time to re-establish after a rotation, a well-timed fake often reaches the B platform before the defence resets. What utility matters most on B site? The Heaven smoke is non-negotiable — the platform sees the entire site and both entrances. After that: a Sandbags/Short smoke to split the site, a molotov for Toxic/Pillar close positions, and a spare smoke to re-block Heaven for the post-plant. Without Heaven denied, B executes fail against even average defences. How should CTs use aggression on Overpass? Early and purposefully: a Monster check or Toilets peek at round start costs little and reveals the T-side's shape. Overpass punishes passive CTs because Ts can build map control quietly through tunnels and Connector — the defence's counter is taking information before the T default settles, then resetting to standard holds. What are the main routes into each bombsite? A site: the Long park lane, the Toilets corridor from mid, and the A Short stairs. B site: the Monster tunnel from T spawn, B Short via Construction/Water, and the Water canal itself for boosts and late-round surprises. CTs rotate between the halves through Connector or across Heaven. When was Overpass in the Active Duty pool? Overpass joined CS:GO in December 2013 and was an Active Duty fixture for roughly a decade. In CS2 it was rotated out for Dust 2 in April 2024, brought back in July 2025 in place of Anubis, and rotated out again for Cache on July 8, 2026 — its current reserve status. Is Overpass CT or T sided? Across 1752 tracked pro matches, CTs win 56% of rounds on Overpass versus 47% for Terrorists, making it a CT-sided map at the professional level. How often is Overpass picked in pro CS2? Overpass has an average pick rate of 5.8% and a ban rate of 23.3% across 25 tracked teams. The pistol round is won 51.5% of the time in the map's data sample.
